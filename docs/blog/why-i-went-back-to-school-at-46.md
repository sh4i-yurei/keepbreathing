# Why I went back to school at 46

I went back to school because I wanted the second half of my life to be about me.

That's the short answer. The longer one starts with the divorce and everything that came after it. Rebuilding the shape of your life at 44 is its own kind of work, and while I was doing it I ended up back on a kitchen line because that's what I knew. I was good at it, the money came in, and it wasn't going to be the rest of my life. Standing there with the same burns on the same forearm I've had since I was 22, running food for a room full of people I'd never see again, I already knew the answer. I could keep doing this and I'd keep being good at it. Nothing about it was going to change. No matter how good the food got or how well service ran, this wasn't the life I wanted.

What I wanted was different. Work that used my head, where the hard part was thinking, not moving. Something I could keep getting better at for years without hitting a ceiling, that was rewarding in a way kitchens never were for me.

Most of my adult life has been in service to other people. That's not a complaint, it's just the shape it took, and for a long time I thought it was the only shape it could take. I don't think that anymore. The first half of my life took care of a lot of people who needed me. The second half I'm giving to what I actually want to do.

The kids were part of it too. Six of them, mine and my stepkids together, and all of them watching. Whatever I told them about believing in themselves and staying with something hard was going to weigh against what I'd actually done with my own life. If I wasn't willing to persevere, I had no business asking them to.

Underneath that was something harder to admit. I'd sold myself short for a long time. When I first started school I still half-believed computers were something I loved on the side, not something a person like me built a life around. Coming back to a kitchen after the divorce told me otherwise. I'd already survived more than I ever gave myself credit for. Whatever was still in the way of finishing what I'd started was mostly me.

So I went back. Computer Networking and Cybersecurity at Hinds, dual A.A.S., graduating December 2026. Mississippi State after that for the bachelor's, then graduate work in machine learning and AI. That's the plan and I'm keeping to it.

I don't have a clean ending for this. I'm in the middle of it. Certifications are stacking, my GPA is where I want it, and there's still a semester ahead. What I know is I'm not going back to a kitchen, and the next stretch of my life isn't going to be built around what anyone else needs from me. This one's mine.
