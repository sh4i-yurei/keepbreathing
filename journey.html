The Long Way Here▋

The road from a 386 in Vicksburg to a career in cybersecurity.

1979

origin

Born in Memphis in 1979 and grew up in Vicksburg, Mississippi. I remember the world before the internet. It was VHS tapes and cassette decks and Saturday morning cartoons. I played my Atari 2600 until the original Nintendo came out, and my parents got me one of those too. I was a teenager when the internet showed up for regular people, and I still remember what it felt like to watch something that big arrive.

#origin
1993

first contact

My first machine was a 386 running DOS and Windows 3.1, and I spent my time hunched over that prompt figuring out the right commands to make it do what I wanted. Prodigy and then AOL turned the internet into something an ordinary house could dial into, and I was the kid using it to talk to people on the other side of the world and dig up things that were not easy for a kid to reach. Around the same age my dad gave me Radio Shack electronics kits and a camera. All three of those stuck.

#tech
teens

fixing things

I was taking computers apart and fixing them for other people before I could drive, and I never stopped no matter what else I was doing. What pulled at me was never whether a thing ran. It was what was holding it together underneath.

#hardware
the 2000s

the kitchen years

Cooking started as a kid in my memaw's kitchen and became the thing I fell back on, because I was good at it and kitchens always hire. I went to culinary school and worked my way up to sous chef in the high-end casino kitchens. The part I lived for was go time, when the kitchen got slammed and there was no room left to fake anything. What wore me down was never the work. It was being surrounded by people who did not care about it the way I did.

#kitchen
along the way

the diner

At one diner I managed I ended up wiring the whole place myself, from the router and switch out to the POS terminals at the bar and wait stations and the thermal printers back in the kitchen. The cook and the tech guy were the same guy. Tech was never something I left. It just kept riding along under everything else.

#networking
2014

first try at tech

A couple of years after culinary school I went back to Faulkner State to try computer networking. I struggled with it, and I had almost no support to lean on, so I talked myself into believing I was not capable of it and switched to commercial art with a focus in digital media. I had not figured out yet how I actually learn, and I read that confusion as a limit instead of a gap. So I walked away from the one thing that had always been mine.

#networking
2014-17

the camera

While I was in school for commercial art I started turning the camera my dad had given me into a business, shooting weddings, family portraits, and real estate for people selling houses. After my first big wedding I put in a twelve hour day and then stayed up all night cutting a slideshow for the family's crawfish boil the next morning. Watching them tear up over it, all the smiles and the joy on their faces, is still one of the best moments I have had at work. I was getting the business off the ground when I got divorced at the end of 2017 and had to sell all my gear.

#photography #business

the answer

Later I found out I'm autistic, and getting that answer made a lot of things easier. It explained how I actually learn, and why "not capable" had been the wrong read. The problem was never a limit. It was a gap I could close once I understood it. That is a big part of why I was able to come back.

#neurodivergent
2018

the tell

I started at Waitr driving deliveries and got moved up to tech support lead once my manager saw what I could do. I was troubleshooting the delivery platform, sorting out point-of-sale problems at the restaurants, and training new drivers. Tech kept surfacing whether I planned on it or not.

#support
2020

the road

For a while I inspected properties for mortgage lenders across rural Mississippi, verifying occupancy and documenting condition, which leaned on the same eye photography had given me. Then delivery driving, which I still do while I go to school. I keep a 4.8 rating across thousands of orders, and the deliveries I like best are the older folks on walkers, where I carry the groceries inside and set them down in the kitchen.

#operations
2024

back to it, for real

I enrolled at Hinds for Computer Networking and Cybersecurity, this time to learn it from the ground up instead of staying self-taught around the edges. Cisco labs, subnetting, Windows Server, Python. I am carrying a 3.5, I made Phi Theta Kappa, and I graduate this December. This time it is sticking.

#networking #security
2026

this site

I hand-coded this whole site myself, no frameworks, to prove I actually understand what I am building. At home I run a three-subnet network on a MikroTik RB5009UG router and a CSS326 switch, with a five-node Raspberry Pi cluster handling DNS, Tailscale, and other services. A Bachelor's in Cybersecurity at Mississippi State comes after Hinds.

#dev #homelab
next

the hard stuff

Security work first, then machine learning and AI. I would rather understand how something works than fake my way through it, so I am doing it in order.

#future
